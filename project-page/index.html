The workflow

Different stages.
Different ways of reasoning.

Anatomical liver resection planning moves from understanding complex anatomy to balancing safety criteria and preparing for surgery. LiverPlan adapts visualization and interaction to the cognitive demands of each stage.

Stage 01 / Tumor-Vessel Spatial Analysis

Enhanced visualization of
tumor-vessel spatial relationships.

Explore anatomy at different levels.

Whole-liver and liver-segment representations expose tumor extent and segment morphology while retaining the surrounding anatomical context.

In LiverPlan 20 s

Switch between anatomical representations to inspect the tumor and individual liver segments.

Reveal internal tumor-vessel relationships.

Adjustable vessel thickness reveals structures that would otherwise be occluded. Hue-preserving rendering retains diagnostic colors through overlapping surfaces.

In LiverPlan 18 s

Reduce vessel thickness to reveal internal tumor-vessel relationships while retaining the surrounding vascular context.

Select and revise candidate target segments.

Linked selection controls and the 3D anatomy help surgeons identify the target segments for resection planning.

In LiverPlan 13 s

Select and revise candidate target segments, with the selection panel and 3D anatomy staying linked.

Stage 02 / Interactive Resection Planning

Real-time, embedded visual feedback
on critical safety criteria.

Critical safety criteria stay within the focused 3D view, supporting multi-constraint reasoning as surgeons refine a resection plan.

In LiverPlan 24 s

As a resection plane moves, the remnant outline changes color to flag a Functional Liver Remnant threshold violation and support further refinement.

  • Functional Liver RemnantA color-coded outline and numerical indicator show the remaining liver volume.
  • Resection MarginA connector line or surface distance map shows tumor-to-plane distance.
  • Target Segment Complete RemovalDynamic contours indicate when a resection plane intersects a target segment.

Stage 03 / Plane-Vessel Intersection Analysis

Enhanced spatial understanding
of the resection plan.

Focus+Context plane-vessel intersection visualization shows the vascular implications of the finalized plan, supporting an anticipatory mental model for surgery preparation.

In LiverPlan 12 s

Rotate the finalized anatomy to inspect its vascular context. The magnified design view shows the intersection collars in detail.

Design study

Grounded in clinical reasoning.

Clinical collaboration shaped the workflow before it shaped the interface.

8 monthsof iterative design with
2 hepatobiliary surgeons

We worked with an attending surgeon and a resident surgeon to understand how anatomical discovery, constraint reasoning, and surgery preparation unfold in practice.

12 weeks

Clinical Discovery

Interviews, surgical planning seminars, and observation of a live liver resection surgery revealed clinical practices and visualization bottlenecks in desktop tools.

20 weeks

Iterative Design & Refinement

Repeated design sessions, consultations, and expert validation translated these findings into a stage-adaptive visual analytics framework.

From clinical practice to visualization tasks.

The Multi-Level Typology of Abstract Visualization Tasks guided the translation of clinical findings into a cognitively grounded workflow, informing three design goals and five design requirements.

Evaluation

Evaluated with
hepatobiliary surgeons.

Eight surgeons completed controlled planning tasks using LiverPlan and a configured 3D Slicer desktop baseline in a counterbalanced within-subjects study.

51.69%Lower task completion timeOverall planning tasks
32.63%Lower perceived workloadWeighted NASA-TLX
76.25System Usability Scale scoreCompared with 38.44 for the desktop baseline

Results are relative to the desktop baseline on controlled planning tasks across four clinical cases and two complexity levels.
